\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have argued that groundedness in retrieval-augmented generation is an aggregation problem spanning the pipeline rather than a property of generation alone. Between retrieval and generation, a model decides which claims to privilege and how to reconcile conflict. A system can cite its context faithfully while still distorting the balance of perspectives that context contains. There are multiple defensible ways to combine evidence, such as different interpretations or priorities, normative conclusions or even document format. Social choice theory offers a framework for understanding how they should be combined. Our experiments on \textsc{ConflictingQA} show that models are sensitive to expertise signals without tracking human judgements of expertise. These findings return to the question in our title: asking whether an answer is supported by its context is not enough. We should also ask why the question was asked, and what that purpose demands of the way evidence is combined.
