\begin{abstract}
Groundedness in retrieval-augmented generation is usually defined as fidelity to the retrieved context. We argue that this definition is incomplete. A model can faithfully report every perspective it retrieves while still producing an answer that privileges only a subset of the perspectives represented in the evidence. Between retrieval and generation, the model must decide which perspectives to foreground and how to reconcile conflicts among them, yet these interpretive decisions are invisible to conventional measures of groundedness. We identify \emph{unrepresentative aggregation} as a distinct failure mode of interpretive RAG: an answer may be faithful to its retrieved evidence while misrepresenting the perspectives that evidence contains. We use the existing \textsc{ConflictingQA} dataset \citep{wan-etal-2024-evidence} for proof-of-concept experiments that vary the presentation of conflicting perspectives while holding their substantive claims fixed. The results provide preliminary evidence that models' answers are sensitive to how perspectives are balanced, presented, and ordered. We argue that interpretive RAG should therefore be evaluated not only for fidelity to retrieved evidence, but also for the representativeness with which it aggregates conflicting perspectives.
\end{abstract}
